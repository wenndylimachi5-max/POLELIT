Why it matters

Why is it so hard to choose where to land?

At the lunar south pole the Sun never rises high. A nearby mountain can leave a site in darkness for days, and the Earth, needed for direct communication, keeps hiding behind the horizon. Deep craters stay in permanent shadow and may trap water ice.

FAQ

Common questions

Where does the data come from?

Sun and Earth positions come from NASA JPL Horizons, every hour of 2027. The terrain comes from LOLA elevation maps (Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter) published by NASA PGDA: an 80 m map for the horizon, 5 m maps for slope and a 20 m map of permanent shadows.

Why 2 meters above the ground?

That is the typical height of a lander's solar panels, the same height used in the reference study by Gläser et al. (2017).

What is a permanently shadowed region?

A place, usually inside a crater, that never receives direct sunlight. They are extremely cold and may hold water ice, one of the main reasons to explore the south pole.

Why is the lunar sky black even in daylight?

The Moon has no atmosphere to scatter sunlight. For the same reason there is no refraction: the horizon is set only by the terrain and the curvature of the Moon.

How accurate is it?

Average sunlight on the ridge is 88.2% vs 88.0% published by Gläser et al. (2017), and the longest night on the Shackleton rim is 64 h vs 65 h. Differences remain because we simulate one year (2027) with an 80 m map, while the paper averages 20 years with finer maps.
